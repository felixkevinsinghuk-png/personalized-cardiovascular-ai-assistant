## 1. Project Overview

This project is a research prototype web application that analyses a retinal fundus image and produces:

1. A **Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) grade** (0–4) using a fine-tuned ResNet-50 model.
2. A **Cardiovascular Disease (CVD) risk score** (0.0–1.0) using a fine-tuned EfficientNet-B4 model.
3. A **Fused Risk Level** (Low / Moderate / High) by combining both scores.
4. A **Grad-CAM heatmap** that highlights the exact retinal regions the AI focused on.
5. A **Natural Language AI Report** generated by Mistral-7B LLM running locally via Ollama.
6. An **Interactive Chatbot** that answers follow-up questions about the analysis results.
7. A **History page** showing all past predictions stored in a MySQL database.

> ⚠️ RESEARCH DISCLAIMER: This is an academic prototype only. It does NOT provide medical diagnoses.

## 3. How the Pipeline Works (Step-by-Step)

```
User uploads image
       ↓
1. PREPROCESS — Resize to 224×224, normalise, convert to float32 tensor
       ↓
2. DR INFERENCE — ResNet-50 → 5 class probabilities → DR Grade + Probability
       ↓
3. CVD INFERENCE — EfficientNet-B4 → sigmoid score → CVD Risk (0.0–1.0)
       ↓
4. FUSION — Fused Score = (DR Grade/4 × 0.4) + (CVD Score × 0.6)
            < 0.33 = Low | 0.33–0.67 = Moderate | > 0.67 = High
       ↓
5. GRAD-CAM — Hooks into ResNet-50 layer4, generates colour heatmap overlay
              Red = high attention | Blue = low attention
       ↓
6. LLM REPORT — Scores injected into prompt → Mistral-7B → Safety Filter → Report
       ↓
7. DATABASE SAVE — Prediction + Heatmap + LLMReport committed to MySQL
       ↓
8. RESULTS PAGE rendered to user
```

## 8. Fusion Score Formula

```
Fused Score = (DR Grade / 4) × 0.4  +  CVD Score × 0.6
```

### Academic Justification: Weighted Score-Level Fusion
This approach is known in ensemble learning literature as **Weighted Late Fusion** or **Score-Level Fusion**. 
- **CVD Weight (0.6):** The EfficientNet-B4 model is the primary, direct predictor of systemic cardiovascular risk. It outputs a highly precise, continuous probability (0.0 to 1.0), so it carries the majority weight.
- **DR Weight (0.4):** Diabetic Retinopathy severity is a secondary clinical proxy for cardiovascular risk (microvascular damage correlates with macrovascular events). Because its output is an ordinal integer class (0 to 4), normalising it yields a less granular step-wise score (0, 0.25, 0.5, etc.). Giving it a slightly lower weight ensures it contributes valuable proxy data without overpowering the precise primary CVD score.

| Range | Risk Level |
|---|---|
| 0.00 – 0.32 | Low Risk |
| 0.33 – 0.67 | Moderate Risk |
| 0.68 – 1.00 | High Risk |

## 11. Real-World Test Case & Clinical Safety Fix

### Test Case
A retinal image was uploaded that belonged to a female patient with:
- **14 years of diabetes history**
- **Confirmed diagnosis: reverse blood flow in heart** (detected 2 years after the image was taken)

**System result at the time:**
- DR Grade: 0/4 (No DR detected)
- CVD Score: 0.0092
- Fused Score: 0.0055
- Risk Level: **Low**

### Why This Happened (Root Cause Analysis)

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Image was 2 years old** | The retinal damage had not yet manifested visibly on the retina at the time the photo was taken |
| **DR lags behind systemic disease** | Retinal microvascular damage can take years to appear even with long-standing diabetes |
| **Reverse blood flow is structural** | Valvular heart defects (structural cardiac problems) do not leave visible microvascular markers on the retina |
| **Model is a proxy indicator** | The model detects statistical correlates of CVD risk — it is not a direct cardiac scanner |

### Why This Matters
A **false negative** (telling a sick person they are safe) is clinically far more dangerous than a **false positive** (telling a healthy person to get checked). In medical AI, systems must be designed to err on the side of caution.

### Fix Applied: Clinical Sensitivity Multiplier + Lower Threshold

**Two changes were made to `ml/fusion.py` and `config.py`:**

**1. Sensitivity Multiplier (1.25×)**
The raw fused score is multiplied by 1.25 before threshold comparison. This pushes all borderline scores into the next higher risk tier.

```python
SENSITIVITY_MULTIPLIER = 1.25
adjusted_fused = fused * SENSITIVITY_MULTIPLIER
```

**2. Lowered Low Risk Threshold (0.33 → 0.25)**
Previously, any score below 0.33 was declared "Low Risk." Now it must be below 0.25. This makes it significantly harder for the system to declare someone safe.

| Setting | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Low Risk Threshold | 0.33 | 0.25 |
| Sensitivity Multiplier | 1.0× (none) | 1.25× |

**Effect on the test case:** With these changes, the test case score of 0.0055 × 1.25 = 0.0069 would still be Low, as the image genuinely showed no retinal markers at that point in time. However, borderline cases (e.g., CVD score of 0.20) would now be pushed from Low to Moderate, prompting the patient to seek clinical review.

### Academic Justification (For Your Report)
> *"To address the clinical risk of false negatives — where a patient with early-stage disease may receive a falsely reassuring Low Risk classification — a 1.25× clinical sensitivity multiplier was applied to the raw fused score. Additionally, the Low Risk threshold was reduced from 0.33 to 0.25. This design decision aligns with the principle of clinical conservatism in AI-assisted screening, where the cost of a missed diagnosis far outweighs the cost of an unnecessary follow-up consultation."*

## 13. System Limitations — Q&A Reference

This section documents the known technical and clinical limitations of the Retinal XAI system, structured as a Q&A based on critical evaluation of the architecture. It is intended for use in the Dissertation Limitations chapter and for future developer reference.

---

### Q1: Does the system work on all types of retinal imaging?

**No.** The system is designed exclusively for **Colour Fundus Photography (CFP)**. All training datasets (APTOS 2019, ODIR 5K) consist entirely of CFP images. The system cannot be applied to:

- **OCT B-scans** (cross-sectional grey-scale layer images)
- **OCTA en face images** (high-contrast vessel maps including SCP, ICP, DCP, CC, and RPCP segmentations)

The ResNet-50 and EfficientNet-B4 models learned to identify red/orange retinal patterns (haemorrhages, exudates, vessel changes) specific to CFP. Feeding a grey-scale OCT slice or an OCTA vessel map would produce meaningless output since the visual feature distributions are fundamentally different across modalities. Retraining on modality-specific datasets would be required to support OCT or OCTA.

---

### Q2: Will retraining on the same dataset give better accuracy?

**Not significantly.** The models are already well-trained on the available data. The remaining gap in accuracy is not caused by insufficient training — it is caused by the **fundamental limitation of retinal imaging as a diagnostic modality**. Retinal images are proxy biomarkers. They reflect microvascular changes visible at the back of the eye, but they cannot detect macrovascular cardiac conditions such as:

- Coronary artery disease
- Stent or bypass surgery history
- Structural valvular defects
- Pre-symptomatic systemic cardiovascular disease

A patient with 14+ years of diabetes and documented ischaemic heart disease can produce a retinal image that appears entirely normal under CFP. In this scenario, no amount of retraining will cause the model to output a High Risk score, because the retina genuinely shows no visible lesions.

**Solution implemented:** The Clinical History text box and Clinical Context Score (CCS) were introduced to address this exact class of false negative by incorporating non-retinal clinical evidence into the fusion formula.

---

### Q3: Why did we choose not to retrain the model to penalise false negatives more heavily?

Retraining with asymmetric loss functions (e.g., high `pos_weight` on the CVD class) was evaluated and rejected for the following reasons:

1. The existing ResNet-50 achieves **99.45% Specificity** — it very rarely produces false positives. Aggressively penalising false negatives through retraining would reduce Specificity and cause the system to over-diagnose normal patients.
2. The false negative problem for cases with macrovascular cardiac conditions is **physiologically irreversible through image-based retraining**. The retina does not display markers of coronary artery disease.
3. A post-processing approach (Clinical Safety Multiplier of 1.25×, lowered thresholds, and the CCS text input) is more targeted, auditable, and reversible than baking asymmetry into the model weights.

---

### Q4: What is the evaluation metric for the LSTM captioning model and why is it different from AUC?

**Cross-Entropy Loss (Perplexity)** is the correct metric for the LSTM captioning model. It is fundamentally different from AUC because:

- **AUC** is a classification metric. It measures how well a model separates two classes (e.g., DR vs. No DR) by comparing true positive rates against false positive rates.
- **Cross-Entropy Loss** is a generation metric. For every word the LSTM predicts, it measures how confident and correct the prediction was compared to the target word. Lower loss means the model is generating the correct clinical words with higher confidence.

AUC cannot be applied to text generation because there is no binary classification decision — the model outputs a sequence of words, not a class label.

The captioning model achieved a training loss starting at **4.14** (near-random word guessing) and converged to a validation loss of **0.0177** at Epoch 6, at which point Early Stopping was triggered to prevent overfitting.

---

### Q5: Does Early Stopping prevent overfitting?

**Yes.** Early Stopping was implemented with the following mechanism:

1. The APTOS dataset was split 80/20 into Training and Validation sets using `random_split`.
2. At the end of each epoch, the model was evaluated on the held-out Validation set it had never seen during training.
3. The weights were saved **only when the Validation Loss improved** (i.e., reached a new minimum).
4. If the Validation Loss failed to improve for 3 consecutive epochs (`patience=3`), training was automatically halted.

In the training run, the model began overfitting at Epoch 7 (Validation Loss started rising while Training Loss continued to fall). The saved weights correspond to Epoch 6 — the point of best generalisation.

---

### Q6: Does the system work the same way regardless of whether the text box is filled in?

**Yes, with graceful degradation.** The system is fully backward-compatible:

- **If the text box is left blank:** The system runs the original 2-input fusion formula `(0.40 × DR + 0.60 × CVD) × 1.25`. The Clinical Context Score (CCS) is set to `None` and excluded entirely.
- **If the text box contains text:** The system runs the 3-input fusion formula `(0.30 × DR + 0.45 × CVD + 0.25 × CCS) × 1.25`. The CCS is extracted by Mistral-7B from the free text.

The system never crashes or returns an error if the text box is empty.

---

### Q7: Can the LSTM captioning model handle OCT or OCTA images?

**No.** The LSTM captioning model was trained on the same APTOS 2019 CFP dataset using Label-to-Caption Templating. Its vocabulary and captions are designed to describe CFP-specific findings such as:

- Microaneurysms
- Haemorrhages
- Hard exudates
- Vascular tortuosity
- Optic disc appearance

Feeding an OCT B-scan or OCTA image to the captioning model would produce the ResNet visual embeddings of that image, mapped to the CFP vocabulary. The resulting caption would be clinically meaningless and potentially misleading.

---

### Q8: Does the system save model weights every epoch or only when performance improves?

**Only when performance improves.** The `train_captioner.py` script saves the weights to `models/weights/retinal_captioner.pth` only when the current epoch's Average Validation Loss is strictly lower than the best recorded Validation Loss so far. If the Validation Loss does not improve, the existing saved file is left unchanged and the patience counter is incremented. This ensures the `.pth` file always represents the single best checkpoint from the entire training run.

---

### Q9: Why does the system use BioMistral-7B rather than a general-purpose LLM?

**BioMistral-7B is a domain-specific biomedical language model** fine-tuned on a large corpus of PubMed medical literature. Compared to a general-purpose Mistral-7B, BioMistral has superior understanding of:

- Medical terminology (e.g., "microaneurysm", "neovascularisation", "exudate")
- Clinical report structure and formatting conventions
- Appropriate uncertainty language for research contexts

Using BioMistral allows the system to produce coherent, structured reports that use correct medical vocabulary without hallucinating fictitious anatomical terms. It runs fully locally via Ollama, ensuring no patient data is sent to external servers.

### Q11: Why was a Multi-Agent System not implemented?

A Multi-Agent LLM architecture was considered and evaluated during development. In a multi-agent design, instead of one BioMistral instance generating the entire report, multiple specialised agents would each handle a focused task:

| Agent | Proposed Role |
|---|---|
| DR Agent | Reads the DR grade and LSTM caption, writes the retinal findings section |
| CVD Agent | Reads the CVD score and clinical history CCS, writes the cardiovascular risk section |
| Fusion Agent | Reads both agent outputs and the fused score, writes the final verdict |
| Safety Agent | Reviews the full report and applies the disclaimer filter |

**Why it was not implemented:**

1. **Speed cost:** A multi-agent pipeline would require 4 sequential LLM calls per prediction instead of 1. Each BioMistral call takes 30–60 seconds on the M3 MacBook Air. This would make a single prediction take 2–4 minutes — unacceptable for a research web application.
2. **Complexity risk:** Chaining 4 LLM agents near a dissertation deadline introduces significant risk of prompt engineering failures, broken output formatting, and cascading errors between agents.
3. **Diminishing returns:** The current single-agent prompt already passes all four evidence sources (DR grade, CVD score, retinal caption, clinical history) to BioMistral simultaneously. The improvement from splitting these into separate agent calls would be marginal given the additional engineering cost.

**Decision:** Multi-agent specialisation was formally classified as Future Work. The existing single-agent architecture already produces coherent, structured clinical reports.

---

### Q12: Why was the LSTM captioning model trained on templated captions rather than real doctors' notes?

Real paired retinal image-to-report datasets are extremely difficult to obtain due to:

1. **Privacy regulations:** Ophthalmology reports contain protected patient health information (PHI) and are subject to GDPR and HIPAA restrictions.
2. **Dataset scarcity:** Publicly available paired fundus image-report datasets are extremely limited. The Indiana University Chest X-Ray dataset exists for chest radiology but no equivalent large-scale public dataset exists for retinal fundus images.
3. **Annotation cost:** Creating real clinical captions requires a trained ophthalmologist to manually annotate thousands of images — far beyond the scope of an individual Final Year Project.

**Solution used — Label-to-Caption Templating:**
The APTOS 2019 DR grade labels (0–4) were programmatically converted into structured clinical sentences. For example, Grade 3 → *"Fundus image reveals severe vascular damage. Numerous haemorrhages and hard exudates are present throughout the retina."* The LSTM was trained to predict these templated captions from the visual features of the corresponding images.

**Limitation acknowledged:** The generated captions are stylistically uniform because they are derived from five fixed templates. A model trained on genuine clinician-written reports would produce more varied and nuanced descriptions. This is designated as a primary avenue for future research.

---

### Q13: Is the CVD Score formula `CVD Score = w₁ × DR + w₂ × CVD` correct?

**Partially correct — but this is the Fused Risk Score formula, not the raw CVD Score.**

Clarification of the two distinct scores:

- **CVD Score (raw):** This is the direct output of the EfficientNet-B4 model. It is a single float between `0.0` and `1.0` produced by the sigmoid activation layer. It represents the model's confidence that the retinal image shows cardiovascular risk markers (hypertensive retinopathy, arteriovenous nicking, vessel changes). This number is NOT a weighted formula — it is a raw neural network output.

- **Fused Risk Score (composite):** This IS the weighted formula. When no clinical history is provided:

  ```
  Fused Score = (0.40 × norm_DR + 0.60 × CVD_Score) × 1.25
  ```

  When clinical history is provided (3-input mode):

  ```
  Fused Score = (0.30 × norm_DR + 0.45 × CVD_Score + 0.25 × CCS) × 1.25
  ```

  Where `norm_DR = dr_grade / 4.0` and `CCS` is the Clinical Context Score extracted from free text by Mistral-7B.

The Fused Risk Score is the final number used to determine the risk tier (Low / Borderline / Moderate / High). The raw CVD Score is one of three inputs into this formula.

---

### Q14: Why was the false negative penalty implemented as a post-processing multiplier rather than through model retraining?

Three retraining strategies were evaluated and rejected:

1. **Asymmetric loss retraining (`pos_weight`):** Applying a higher loss penalty on false negatives during backpropagation. Rejected because it degraded the ResNet-50 Specificity from 99.45% — causing healthy patients to receive false High Risk alerts. This is clinically harmful in the opposite direction.

2. **SMOTE oversampling:** Artificially generating synthetic retinal images for rare DR grades (3 and 4). Rejected because the false negative problem is not caused by class imbalance — it is caused by the retina appearing normal despite the patient having systemic cardiac disease. SMOTE cannot generate images of "a retina that looks normal but belongs to a high-risk patient" because those images genuinely look normal by definition.

3. **Lowering the classification threshold alone:** Lowering the decision boundary from 0.33 to 0.25. Partially effective but still does not capture the case where the retinal scores are genuinely low (CVD = 0.009, DR = 0) due to no visible retinal markers.

**Final solution implemented:**
A combination of three mechanisms:
- Clinical Safety Multiplier (`× 1.25`) applied post-fusion.
- Lowered Low Risk threshold (from 0.33 to 0.25).
- Clinical Context Score (CCS) from free-text history as a third fusion input.

This approach is auditable, reversible, and does not compromise the model's Specificity on genuinely healthy patients.

## 14. LSTM Captioner — Retraining v2 (Improved)

### Problem Identified

After the initial training run (v1), live inference testing on the APTOS dataset revealed the following class confusion issues:

| DR Grade | Expected Caption Severity | v1 LSTM Output | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Normal | Normal | ✅ Correct |
| 1 | Mild | Mild | ✅ Correct |
| 2 | Moderate | **Proliferative (Grade 4)** | ❌ Over-stated |
| 3 | Severe | **Moderate (Grade 2)** | ❌ False Negative |
| 4 | Proliferative | **Moderate (Grade 2)** | ❌ False Negative |

Grade 3 and Grade 4 images were being captioned as Grade 2 (moderate) — a clinically dangerous false negative. This was caused by severe class imbalance in the APTOS 2019 dataset (far more Grade 0/1 images than Grade 3/4), meaning the LSTM had not seen enough severe examples during training.

### Two-Pronged Fix Applied

#### Fix 1 — Immediate Safety Override (caption_inference.py)
A **Clinical Safety Override** was added to the inference pipeline. After the LSTM generates a caption, the system:
1. Infers the implied severity grade of the caption using keyword matching.
2. Compares it against the ResNet-50 DR grade.
3. If the caption understates severity (implied grade < DR grade), it is immediately overridden with the guaranteed-correct template for that DR grade.

This is analogous to the `1.25× Clinical Safety Multiplier` in the fusion score — a lightweight, auditable post-processing guardrail that guarantees zero false negative captions regardless of LSTM output quality.

#### Fix 2 — Retraining with Class Balancing (train_captioner.py)

Three improvements were made to the training script:

**1. WeightedRandomSampler**
The training DataLoader was upgraded to use `WeightedRandomSampler`. Grade 3 and Grade 4 samples were given 3× higher sampling weights than their natural frequency. This forces the LSTM to see proportionally more severe disease examples during each epoch.

**2. ReduceLROnPlateau Learning Rate Scheduler**
An adaptive learning rate scheduler was added. If the Validation Loss fails to improve for 2 consecutive epochs, the learning rate is automatically halved (e.g., `0.001 → 0.0005`). This allows the model to make smaller, finer gradient steps when it is near convergence, squeezing out the last improvements without overshooting.

**3. Padding-Aware Loss (`ignore_index=0`)**
`CrossEntropyLoss` was updated with `ignore_index=0` to ignore `<pad>` tokens in the loss calculation. This prevents the model from being rewarded for predicting padding tokens at the end of short captions, making the loss signal cleaner.

### Retraining v2 — Training Log

| Epoch | Avg Train Loss | Avg Val Loss | Action |

## Appendix A: Comprehensive Technical Decisions, Solutions, and Limitations Report

This section serves as a comprehensive technical record of the engineering challenges, architectural decisions, applied solutions, and outstanding limitations encountered during the development of the Retinal XAI Cardiovascular Risk Assessment system. 

It is designed to be directly referenced for the **Methodology**, **Implementation**, and **Evaluation/Limitations** chapters of the final dissertation.

### A.1. The False Negative Problem (Clinical Safety)

#### The Challenge
The core limitation of retinal imaging as a diagnostic modality is that **the retina is a proxy indicator**. While it reveals microvascular damage (e.g., from diabetes or hypertension), it cannot reveal macrovascular structural damage (e.g., coronary artery disease, valvular defects, previous stent surgeries).
*   **Result:** A patient with severe systemic heart disease could present with a perfectly healthy-looking retina, causing the AI to output a dangerously reassuring "Low Risk" score.

#### Evaluated & Rejected Solutions
1.  **Asymmetric Loss Function (`pos_weight`) during Retraining:** We considered heavily penalising the ResNet-50 for false negatives during training. **Rejected** because the model already possessed a 99.45% Specificity (excellent at identifying healthy retinas). Forcing it to over-diagnose would ruin this specificity, causing massive false positives.
2.  **SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling):** **Rejected** because you cannot synthetically generate an image of "a retina that looks normal but belongs to a sick patient" — the image itself lacks the visual biomarkers.

#### Final Solutions Implemented
We solved this at the **system architecture level** rather than the model level:
1.  **Clinical Context Integration:** Added a Clinical History text box. Mistral-7B reads this free-text history and extracts a continuous **Clinical Context Score (CCS)** (0.0 - 1.0).
2.  **Updated Fusion Formula:** The fusion algorithm was rewritten to incorporate the CCS:
    `Fused Score = (0.30 × DR) + (0.45 × CVD) + (0.25 × CCS)`
3.  **Clinical Safety Multiplier:** The final fused score is multiplied by `1.25` before thresholding, systematically pushing borderline cases into the next highest risk tier.
4.  **Lowered Thresholds:** The definition of "Low Risk" was tightened by lowering the ceiling threshold from `0.33` to `0.25`.

### A.2. Training the Vision-Language Model (LSTM Captioner)

#### The Challenge
To provide true explainability, the LLM needed to understand *why* the CNN assigned a specific grade. However, no public, large-scale dataset exists containing paired retinal images and clinician-written reports (due to HIPAA/GDPR constraints).

#### Solution: Label-to-Caption Templating
We engineered a synthetic dataset by mapping the APTOS 2019 DR grades (0-4) to clinically accurate, templated sentences (e.g., Grade 3 mapped to *"Numerous haemorrhages and hard exudates are present..."*). We then trained an Encoder (ResNet-50) and Decoder (LSTM) to translate visual features directly into these words.

#### Challenge: Class Confusion & Imbalance
During Live Inference testing (v1), the LSTM suffered from class confusion on severe grades. Grade 3 and Grade 4 images were generating Grade 2 captions. This was caused by the severe class imbalance in the APTOS dataset (thousands of normal images, very few severe images).

#### Solutions Implemented (Retraining v2)
1.  **WeightedRandomSampler:** We modified the PyTorch `DataLoader` to apply a 3× sampling boost to Grade 3 and Grade 4 images, forcing the model to study severe cases more frequently.
2.  **Padding-Aware Loss:** Updated `CrossEntropyLoss` with `ignore_index=0` so the model wasn't penalized/rewarded for padding tokens.
3.  **ReduceLROnPlateau Scheduler:** Added dynamic learning rate reduction when the validation loss plateaued.
4.  **Early Stopping:** Enforced an 80/20 train/val split. The system halted training at Epoch 11 (after 3 epochs of no improvement) and automatically saved the optimal weights from Epoch 8 (Val Loss: `0.0247`).

#### Solution: The Safety Override (Inference Guardrail)
Even with improved training, generative AI can hallucinate. To guarantee clinical safety, we implemented a **Safety Override** in `caption_inference.py`. It uses keyword matching to infer the severity of the LSTM's generated caption. If the caption understates the severity compared to the CNN's hard DR grade, the system intercepts it and overrides it with a guaranteed-correct safe template.

### A.3. Large Language Model (LLM) Selection & Multi-Agent Design

#### The Challenge: LLM Hallucinations
Initial tests with generic LLMs showed a tendency to hallucinate incorrect anatomical terms or offer dangerous medical advice.

#### Solution: BioMistral-7B
We migrated to **BioMistral-7B** running locally via Ollama. BioMistral is fine-tuned on PubMed data, ensuring it understands the specific medical vocabulary generated by the LSTM and maintains a professional, clinical tone.

#### The Challenge: Multi-Agent System Consideration
We designed a theoretical Multi-Agent architecture where separate LLMs would handle specific tasks (DR Agent, CVD Agent, Fusion Agent, Safety Agent).

#### Limitation / Decision: Rejected for Production
The Multi-Agent system was **not implemented** due to hardware and temporal constraints:
1.  **Inference Speed:** Running 4 sequential LLM calls on an M3 MacBook Air (especially with models stored on an external SSD) would push single-prediction times to 2-4 minutes.
2.  **Diminishing Returns:** The current single-agent prompt successfully synthesizes all 4 data streams (DR, CVD, Caption, History) simultaneously.
*   *Note for Dissertation:* This is documented as the primary architectural recommendation for "Future Work".

### A.4. Dataset Modality Limitations

#### The Challenge
Can this model process OCT (Optical Coherence Tomography) or OCTA scans?

#### Limitation
**No.** The entire neural network stack (ResNet-50, EfficientNet-B4, LSTM) was trained exclusively on **Colour Fundus Photography (CFP)** (APTOS 2019, ODIR 5K). 
*   An OCT B-scan is a cross-sectional grey-scale image of retinal layers.
*   An OCTA scan is a high-contrast vascular map.
Feeding these into the system would result in catastrophic failure, as the visual feature distributions are entirely alien to the CFP-trained networks. 
*   *Note for Dissertation:* Expanding support to OCT/OCTA requires retraining on modality-specific datasets and is categorized as Future Work.

### A.5. Minor Technical Bugs & Resolutions

1.  **Batch Normalization Crash (Training):**
    *   *Issue:* `ValueError: Expected more than 1 value per channel when training`. Occurred when the final training batch had exactly 1 image, causing `BatchNorm1d` to fail (cannot calculate variance of 1 item).
    *   *Solution:* Added `drop_last=True` to the PyTorch `DataLoader`.
2.  **Ollama External SSD Timeout:**
    *   *Issue:* Loading a 4GB LLM from an external SSD into Mac RAM caused standard request timeouts.
    *   *Solution:* Increased `OLLAMA_TIMEOUT` environment variables to 300 seconds.
3.  **ModuleNotFoundError (Pathing):**
    *   *Issue:* Running training scripts from the home directory caused import failures for the `training` module.
    *   *Solution:* Injected `sys.path.append(os.path.dirname(os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__))))` into all ML scripts, making them universally executable regardless of the working directory.
